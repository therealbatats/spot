Could SPHEREx really see Planet X?

Probably not, except perhaps in the most favourable case. Planet Nine would shine only by faint reflected sunlight. Brown & Batygin (2016) put it at about visual magnitude 22 to 25; their 2021 update finds a closer orbit (about 380 AU) and says its brightest cases could already have been detected by existing surveys. SPHEREx is designed to reach magnitude 18.4 (AB) or fainter per wavelength channel at 2 µm after its full two-year survey (Crill et al. 2020, Table 1), and a single six-month pass is shallower. Visible and infrared magnitudes are different scales and the planet's infrared brightness depends on its unknown atmosphere, so this comparison is approximate, but a gap of 3.5 magnitudes or more is a factor of 25 or more in brightness. Our simulated dot is drawn much brighter than a real one would be, so you can learn what its motion looks like. Sources: see How it works.
